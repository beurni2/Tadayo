Add new questions at the end, each with a default and the stage that needs the answer. The questions are numbered FQ1 onwards; the spec already uses Q01 to Q30 for its acceptance tests.

Rows move only when the first days end, on their last confirmation or on a leave that leaves only confirmed members (FQ102), which every member sees anyway since every day then has a date, and when a membership change applies, at its start (FQ81). The fixture's first days propose weekdays in join order, Monday to the first to join, as spec 05's examples do, so the frames' order and the join order agree (a contract test ties them).

- **FQ99. - **FQ100. A join in a club's first days once a day is confirmed.** FQ55's reading makes it a membership change, which every active member confirms (FQ56). - **FQ101. - **FQ102. The other readings, keeping each member's choices across a revision that changes `days_per_member`, or freeing a leaver's confirmed day for someone else, would keep a choice made for another size of club, or confirm a day twice in one written-once week. Either is a rule of B2's schedule commands, and changes nothing stored before it applies.
    - Needed by: B2.
    - Since `api/b2-first-days-roster`, built as this default (`packages/domain/src/schedule/roster-change.ts` `firstDaysAfter`, `settle.ts`). "The days the revision proposes for them, which nobody holds" is read as: spec 05's days for the new member's place when nobody holds them, else the first free days from Monday. A revision that changes `days_per_member` writes its week beside the earlier one (`db/migrations/0013_first_days_revision.sql`), so what was proposed before stays on record. Two cases this default doesn't reach are FQ123 and FQ124.
    - Since PR #87 (`api/b2-new-member-day`), a leaver's confirmed day stays `taken` in the proposal with the leaver as its `owner`, as above, but is no longer in the schedule in force: `Schedule.current` holds only the days of members still in the club, so the club's week never shows a member who joins later the leaver's name (its review's finding P1; `packages/domain/src/schedule/read.ts` `readSchedule`). ## Raised by B2's schedule slice (`api/b2-schedule`, PR #68's review)

A member chooses or confirms a day, gets the proposal back with other members' names, then leaves, starts deleting their account, or the club is suspended, and sends the same request again under its key.     - The cost of the default: after a first person joins, everyone approves once more for the people still to come, though nothing about those people changed.
    - Needed by: B1's close, before the trial's first club invites more than one person at a time.
- **FQ121. What a leave does to the club's open roster proposal.** A proposal lists the club's members and the people proposed (spec 04, T7). When a member leaves while one is open, it names someone no longer there, and no source says what becomes of it or of the people already invited.
    - Default: the leaver's own candidates end, since only a candidate's proposer makes its link.     - Needed by: B1's close.
- **FQ122. Leaving a suspended club.** FQ80's default gives a member nothing of a club moderation has suspended: every route answers the neutral not-found. Spec 22 says leaving is always an explicit choice of the member, and doesn't say whether a suspension holds it.

    - Needed by: B6, with moderation's suspension.

## Raised by spike S2's preparation (`api/b2-spike-prepare`, PR #111's review)

The coordinator reserved FQ138 at 23:48 UTC on 2026-11-22.

- **FQ138. A photo within 64 megapixels whose decode would hold the whole image, or more than any header shows.** D09 caps a photo at 20 MiB and 64 megapixels. Most decodes the worker runs stream, a few rows at a time. Five kinds hold the whole image before they give a row:
    - a JPEG whose data comes in more than one scan (progressive, or each component in a scan of its own) keeps every coefficient until its last scan;
    - an interlaced PNG keeps every pixel until its last pass;
    - a lossless WebP keeps every pixel, at 4 bytes;
    - a lossy WebP whose alpha is coded losslessly keeps its alpha plane and the lossless decoder's 4 bytes a pixel;
    - an animated WebP keeps a canvas of 4 bytes a pixel, and composites each frame onto another.

    Within D09 those reach 610 MiB counted (an animated WebP), and PR #111's first two reviews measured the worker's process at up to 776 MiB during one preparation, which the worker's 512 MB Machine can't hold (`docs/plans/api-b2-spike-prepare.md` section 4.4). Even a WebP of a few hundred bytes can name a 64-megapixel canvas. PR #111's third review then found holds no header shows: a PNG's compressed text, inflated as its header is read (one `zTXt` of 49 MiB in a file of 50 KB took the process to 398 MiB); a lossless WebP's Huffman tables (488 MiB from a 1-megapixel image of 65,536 groups); a WebP of millions of chunks (2.6 million empty ones held 400 MiB) and a JPEG of thousands of scans (2,688 took 20 seconds in one step no timeout interrupts); its fourth review, a JPEG that hides its scans from a count behind a reserved marker, which libjpeg passes over without its length where a restart is due (881 hidden scans took 3.8 seconds in the session). And a long-lived process keeps much of what its allocator freed, so photos that each pass every check grow the worker photo by photo. The app never sends any of these: it sends a baseline JPEG of at most 4096 pixels a side, or a screenshot's PNG, never a WebP (ADR-014), and a 4096-pixel progressive JPEG would still fit.
    - Default (Claude Code's), four parts:
        1. The worker decodes no photo itself. Each photo is prepared in a process of its own (`services/worker/src/image-process.ts` `prepareIsolated`, `services/worker/src/image-child.ts`), which answers and exits, so whatever it held goes back to the system. The worker reads that process's peak memory every 10 ms and kills it past 352 MiB (`PEAK_BYTES`): the photo is refused as `too_many_pixels`. The process asks the kernel to kill it first if the Machine runs out (`oom_score_adj` 1000), and one the kernel kills that way is refused the same way. Past 150 seconds (`DEADLINE_SECONDS`), it is killed and the preparation tried again, as for a decode's own timeout.
        2. Before anything is decoded, a JPEG of more than 100 scans (`MAX_JPEG_SCANS`) or with a reserved marker (0x02 to 0xBF) before its end, or a WebP of more than 1,000 chunks (`MAX_WEBP_CHUNKS`), is refused as `unsupported_type`: no encoder makes one, and an encoder's progressive JPEG has about 10 scans. A WebP whose RIFF size isn't its length is refused as `unreadable`.
        3. As before, a photo whose decoder would hold more than 128 MiB at once is refused as `too_many_pixels` (`services/worker/src/image.ts` `MAX_HELD_BYTES`, `heldBytes`). A WebP's kind is read from its chunks (`webpCoding`), one whose kind can't be read is counted as an animation, and so is one that names any frame.
        4. Measured in the photo's own process (section 4.4): the largest photos of each kind the checks take peak at 249 to 324 MiB (an interlaced PNG at the 128 MiB budget is the highest), photos the size the app sends at 137 to 148 MiB, and every hostile file above is refused, the PNG texts and the Huffman tables by the peak, the chunks and the scans by their count or the reserved marker. The worker itself is at 96 MiB with nothing to do and rises by up to 92 MiB over many photos before it levels off (memory freed but not yet given back, not a leak). So the app's photos leave room to spare on the Machine's 488 MiB, and a stranger's photo near the cap can reach its end: then the kernel kills the photo's process, not the worker, and the photo is refused all the same.
    - The other readings: a larger Machine for the worker, which costs more each month and still has a limit; or refusing every kind above outright, which would refuse some images shared from the web that fit.
