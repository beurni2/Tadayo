# Deliberate breakages of `api/b2-new-member-day` at `89d2712`

A new member's day, PR #87: a new member picks free days and they open the week after; nobody else moves or confirms; a fourth member who joins three holding all six days frees the three's second days (the founder's answers to FQ125 to FQ127 and the three-to-four card; FQ129 to FQ132), with migration 0015 (`docs/plans/api-b2-new-member-day.md`). Each rule the PR adds is broken once, on purpose, to see the test that should catch it fail.

**Result: 82 changes at `89d2712`, the PR's last commit. 78 are caught by the test registered for them. Three, R05, K04 and M18, are not caught: equivalent changes that no path through the app can tell apart (below). One, X03, is not caught by the privacy matrix, though the same change is caught in the domain (R19) and on Postgres (R23) (below).** R07's and M05's registered words were changed after the run at the third commit, to those of the test that catches each (below)**

## What is here

- `breakages.py`: the 82 entries. Each names its file, the exact text, its broken form, the suite to run, and words a failing test's name must hold. It changes one place in a scratch worktree at `89d2712` (never the branch's own), runs the suite, and restores the file. The database suites run on a local Postgres 16 made like `ci`'s, with nothing else using it. A breakage of the migration makes that database again from the changed file first, and the database is made again from the true file before the next breakage that isn't the migration's, and after the last.
- `breakages-first-registration.py`: the same entries as first registered, before R07's and M05's words were changed (below).
- `run.sh`: the baseline (each suite unbroken, on a freshly made database), then every entry.
- `run.log`: the run at `89d2712`, all 82. A line reads `CAUGHT` only when the suite failed and a failing test's name holds the registered words.
- `out/`: for each entry, its patch (`<code>.patch`) and the suite's whole output (`<code>.txt`).
- `trial/`: earlier runs, as logs only. `run-1a86ace.log` is the run at the PR's second commit, 69 entries; `run-2a272a7.log` and `run-2a272a7-reregistered.log` are the runs at its third commit, 82 entries, before the fourth commit fixed the test the third run showed passing by chance (below).

## What the runs found

- **At the second commit, `1a86ace`,** 69 entries, not published since the head moved: two real test gaps, both fixed in the third commit and caught since. R19 (a new member's day first opening this week) went uncaught because every test confirmed late in the week, where this week's day is past or under 24 hours away; `change.test.ts` "is that weekday in the week after the confirmation, never this week" now confirms on a Monday morning too. M10 (the database freeing days at every join to four or more) went uncaught because the database's freeing rule was tested only where both its halves hold; `schedule-proposals.test.ts` "keeps in a week after the first days every day of the week before whose member is still in the club" now has a fourth member join with two days free, and refuses a week that frees one.
- **At the third commit, `2a272a7`,** all 82: 77 caught by the registered test at once, and R05, K04 and M18 not caught, each an equivalent change (below). R07 and M05 were caught by a neighbouring test that runs the broken statement and fails because of it. R07 (two who join take the same day) by "frees nothing where the free days are enough for the new members, and nothing in a club of three or fewer", whose first case gives two members who join at once the two free days, one each; the registered test, "gives two who join at once different free days, in the order they joined", gives them their table days, which never meet. M05 (a day made under a later week counts against an earlier one) by "takes a new week whose day is a date with a cancelled day, which then takes the new star's (FQ129)", whose world holds such a day. Registered again with those words and run again, both were caught (`trial/run-2a272a7-reregistered.log`), and this run uses those words. X03 was reported caught there, though the breakage left every week of the club as it was: the matrix's scenario had Sam and Omar join at one instant under a clock that stands still, so their random ids ordered them, and since the third commit that order decided Omar's day. The fourth commit has Omar join a minute later.
- **At the fourth commit, `89d2712`,** all 82, with R07's and M05's words as registered again: 78 caught by the registered test, R05, K04 and M18 not caught (equivalent, below), and X03 not caught. X03 makes a new member's first date fall this week, through the app. The matrix's scenario confirms on a Friday at noon in New York, where that week's Tuesday and Wednesday are past, its Friday's 7 AM is past, and its Saturday's 7 AM is less than 24 hours away, so this week and next give the same first dates and the scenario can't tell the two rules apart. The same change is caught by `change.test.ts` "is that weekday in the week after the confirmation, never this week" (R19, which confirms on a Monday morning too) and on Postgres by `membership-change.test.ts` "writes the days into a new week at once, each the new member's from that weekday next week" (R23). The matrix's judge restates the first date from the rows (`tests/src/privacy/schedule.ts`, finding P6), so a wrong date through the app on another day of the week would be refused there too.

## The equivalent changes, argued

- **R05** counts the free days against the number of new members rather than against spec 05's days for each: `6 - held < fresh` for `6 - held < per * fresh`. The two differ only where `per` is 2 and the free days are at least `fresh` but fewer than `2 × fresh`. `per` is 2 only in a club of two or three, where each of the other members holds at most two days, so `held ≤ 2 × (members − fresh) ≤ 2 × (3 − fresh)`, and the free days, `6 − held`, are at least `2 × fresh`. Both forms are then false: nothing is freed either way.
- **K04** hands the confirmation no last announced day on its weekday. The confirmation's new member picks a free day: no member still in the club has it in the club's latest week, and their days on it from next Monday on are cancelled (a freed day) or were never made. A leaver's held days on it are cancelled from the new member's first date (FQ129), and a leaver's days count for nobody. So the last day announced on that weekday for a member still in the club is always before next Monday, and the first date, never this week (R19), is after it either way. The comment in `newMemberFirstDate`'s test says it is a backstop.
- **M18** lets a member who confirmed in the first days and left contest nothing. In the first days a confirmed day goes into the proposal's week at once, and `schedule_proposal_days_guard` refuses any member a hold on a day already in it ("is confirmed, and a confirmed day is taken (FQ48)"), and a member couldn't have confirmed a day another active member held (FQ55). So when any other member confirms, nobody else holds the leaver's confirmed day, and the leaver's row never meets the contest check.
- **The database's `members >= 4` half** of its freeing rule (`freeing := members >= 4 AND 6 - held < unplaced`) has no entry: while each member holds at most two days, which the database keeps, three members hold at most six, and `6 − held < unplaced` then needs more members than a club of three has. It stays as the rule's own words.
